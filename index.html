What this is

The real Virgo Supercluster — ~110 million light-years across — rendered from published group positions, with the Expanse's worlds pinned on it. Nothing here is to relative scale; star charts never are.

Jump routes

Gold arcs are established in-universe runs, labeled with travel time. Any world can be jumped to directly — routes are flavor, not constraint.

Local systems

Select a world and hit F (or Enter System in its dossier) to drop into its star system — star, planets with procedural surfaces you can fly close to, orbiting moons — with the Ripley at her true smallness against all of it. R or ⏎ Chart flies you back out to where you left the supercluster. Each world's system is retconnable in the DATA block; anything undefined is seeded from the world itself, so every pin has a sky.

Retcon guide

All world data lives in one JSON block at the top of this file's code — <script id="expanse-data"> — separate from the engine. Add, move, or rework a world by editing data, not code:

// copy a world entry, then adjust:
{
  "id": "my-world",
  "name": "My World",
  "class": "desert world",
  "anchor": { "group" | "coords" | "world"
    "type": "coords", "ra": 9.5, "dec": 12.0, "dist": 30
  },
  "starSystem": "binary",   <-- retcon captured here
  "color": "#ffaa44",
  "status": "charted",
  "lore": ["One evocative line.", "A second line."]
}
  • anchor.type "group" pins to a catalog galaxy group's center (add "offset":[x,y,z] in Mly to sit off-center).
  • "coords" places raw J2000 RA hours / Dec degrees / distance Mly.
  • "world" attaches a sub-pin (moon, station) to another world with a local offset.
  • routes draw gold arcs; just name "from", "to" and a "label".
  • systems define each local star system: star (name, class, tint, radius, seed), featured (the world itself — biome, radius, orbit, seed), companion planets, and moonStyles keyed by moon id. Biomes: gas-giant, ocean, terra, garden, desert, ash, barren, ice, lava, storm, toxic, capital, outpost, neon, alien.

Field notes · data corrections

  • Draco plotted as the NGC 4125 group (~70 Mly); NGC 4278 belongs to Coma I — mission figure 50 Mly was unsupported.
  • Antlia plotted as the NGC 3223 group at 109.5 Mly; "40 Mly" was a Megaparsec/Megaly mix-up (it's 40.7 Mpc to the Antlia Cluster).
  • Virgo II / Virgo III centers are midpoints of the Binggeli cloud ranges (68 / 75 Mly). No published group exists for NGC 5643 — it renders as a single marker.
  • System views compress orbital distances for flight (a real interplanetary gap dwarfs the star itself). Body-size hierarchy is honest: star > planet > moon > ship.
  • System texture sizes are also compressed — you could never actually see this much with the naked eye, but the surfaces, bands, and craters are real geometry-relative features, not sprites at honest scale.

Data provenance

Group centers are published J2000 coordinates and distances from catalog values (Wikipedia group infoboxes backed by NED / SIMBAD / HyperLeda, and Richard Powell's Atlas of the Universe group tables). The 160 largest Virgo Cluster members are real catalog entries too — true RA/Dec, morphology, and size, projected at the cluster distance. Other member clouds are an illustrative clustered sample, not a catalog — every pin and world above that layer is Samael's fiction.
